## Choose the length separately from the leg shape

A pair can be wide-legged and cropped, or tapered and ankle-length. “Petite,” “tall” and a number in a product title are useful clues, but they do not tell you exactly where the hem will fall on you. Check the selected size's measurements and the retailer's explanation of its length labels.

## Make a short list of requirements

Write one line for each decision before opening more products:

- **Length:** where you want the hem to sit, plus any measurements you can compare with a pair you already like.
- **Leg shape:** straight, tapered, wide or flared. Keep this separate from length.
- **Waist:** the rise, closure and measurements you want to check in the selected size.
- **Pockets:** whether you want cargo pockets or a simpler layout, and what the listing actually shows.
- **Lining:** whether a lined option is wanted. Do not assume every pair in the collection has the same construction.

For example, “petite-labelled, ankle-length and wide-legged” is more specific than “comfortable khaki pants.” It gives you three things to verify without claiming that any one product will fit you.

## Compare the actual details, not the activity name

“Hiking,” “yoga,” “golf” and “travel” appear in product names. Those labels do not establish that a pair meets your workplace dress code, suits a particular trip or has a performance feature you need.

For an outdoor activity, check the exact feature you care about in the retailer's details. For work, compare the cut and pocket layout with your own dress requirements. Read the composition and care label for that item instead of assuming every khaki pair is cotton or uses the same washing method.

## Finish with the retailer's size and return information

Before ordering, check the exact selected colour and size, the current price and delivery estimate, and how a return would work if the fit is wrong. These examples do not certify current stock or promise a particular feel, durability or fit.
