A $100 gift budget does not necessarily mean $100 of products. Delivery, tax and wrapping can use part of the same money. Decide whether your limit covers the whole purchase before you start making a shortlist.

The method below uses your own estimates. It does not predict a retailer's charges or tell you what you ought to spend.

## First, reserve the extras

Write down one total budget and three **total** estimates: shipping, tax and wrapping. Subtract those estimates before dividing the money between gifts.

**Money for gifts = total budget − total shipping − total tax − total wrapping.**

For example, suppose your total limit is $100. You reserve $15 for delivery, $8 for tax and $5 for wrapping. That leaves **$72 for gifts**. These amounts illustrate the arithmetic; they are not typical fees or a tax calculation.

Use the [gift-budget calculator](/tools/gift-budget) to try your own numbers. It subtracts those total estimates and divides the remainder in cents. If you already included delivery in an all-in item estimate, do not count it again.

## Compare equal shares before you shop

With the same $72 left for gifts:

| Number of gifts | Equal amount per gift | Money left after those equal amounts |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 | $24.00 | $0.00 |
| 5 | $14.40 | $0.00 |
| 7 | $10.28 | $0.04 |

For seven gifts, rounding each amount up to $10.29 would spend $72.03. Keeping each at $10.28 leaves four cents. You can keep that remainder rather than treating it as extra money to spend on every gift.

If your estimated extras already exceed the total budget, there is no money left for items within that limit. For example, $120 of extras against a $100 budget creates a $20 shortfall before buying a gift. Change the plan or the estimates; a negative product budget is not a bargain to hunt for.

## Equal budgets are optional

The calculator gives an equal split, not a rule about relationships. You may want different limits for different recipients. Write those amounts beside the names and check that their sum stays within the money left for gifts.

You can also reserve money for one shared gift. Decide who it is for and whether they would want to share it before treating one purchase as several solved gifts.

## Use the limit to narrow the choice

A price limit is useful after you know what someone would enjoy. A cheap version of the wrong thing still uses the budget.

## Recheck the basket before paying

Replace estimates with the actual retailer totals when you have them. Check whether delivery applies once per order or separately, whether the selected item has extra charges, and whether wrapping is optional. If the total has changed, update your remaining budget before adding another gift.

A delivery estimate is not a promise that a gift will arrive for a particular date. Check the selected item, address and current retailer information separately.
